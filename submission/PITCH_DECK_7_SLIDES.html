1 · Mission Planning Challenge

Why Lunar South Pole Site Selection Is a Multi-Objective Geometry Problem

75°S – 90°S Polar Geometry

1. Grazing Sunlight & Kilometer-Scale Topographic Shadows

  • The Moon's 1.54° axial tilt keeps the Sun near the horizon at the South Pole; crater rims 10–150 km away cast multi-day shadows alongside near-continuous illuminated peaks.
  • Moving just 240–1,200 m laterally or raising a mast from 0.5 m to 10 m can shift solar availability from PSR darkness (0%) to summit illumination (>90%).
  • Partial solar disk clipping (0.266° radius) and 9 lunar solar eclipses (2026–2030) directly reduce solar array power during critical surface operations.

2. Earth Direct-to-Earth (DTE) Libration & Terrain Masking

  • Optical/radio libration swings Earth between ±6.7° in latitude; without an orbital relay, ridges toward 0°E block direct line-of-sight for days at a time.
  • High solar illumination does not guarantee simultaneous Earth DTE: true mission planning requires evaluating Joint Sun + Earth visibility (both_visible_pct ≤ min(sun_time_pct, earth_visible_pct)).
  • Existing static maps show 18.6-year averages at 0 m height, leaving planners unable to test specific 2026–2030 arrival dates, mast heights, or custom pins.
Our Solution Principle: Replace static lookup tables with a deterministic, browser-executable GPU 3D terrain & rover simulator + 360° spherical horizon + hourly ephemeris workspace where every site, traverse, mast height, and mission window can be explored and verified in seconds.

2 · NASA Data & Deterministic Physics Architecture

From Raw NASA PDS LOLA & JPL Horizons Data to Client-Side Geometry

Zero Backend · 9 Static Runtime Assets

A. NASA Data Ingested

  • JPL Horizons (DE441 / MOON_ME): 36,216 hourly sub-solar & sub-Earth points + Earth-Moon distance (2026-11-14 to 2030-12-31).
  • NASA PDS LRO LOLA (LDEM_75S_240M): 240 m/px South Pole stereographic grid (75°S–90°S, R0 = 1,737.4 km).
  • Artemis III 2024 Regions & Site Provenance: 9 region polygons (A3_9regions via LROC QuickMap) + 31 curated sites with NASA/PGDA/LROC/USGS URLs.

B. 360° Horizon & 3D Rover Physics

  • Great-Circle Ray-Marching: 360 azimuths (1°), 480 log-spaced samples (120 m–200 km) with lunar curvature drop and analytic smooth-sphere dip north of 75°S.
  • Dual-Resolution Binary DEMs: dem_fine_240m.i16 (1250×1250, ±150 km) + dem_coarse_1200m_max.i16 (762×762 5×5 block-max to 75°S).
  • Terrain-Normal Rover Kinematics: Bilinear 240 m height & normal sampling (roverPhysics.ts), live pitch/roll projection, 1×/10×/50× gears, and 0.08 km horizon cache.

C. Illumination, Eclipses & Scoring

  • Solar Disk vs. Sun-Time: Separates sun_time_pct (disk_frac > 0) from sun_visible_pct (mean visible disk fraction).
  • Two-Circle Eclipse Obscuration: Models Earth's apparent radius asin(6378.137/dist) overlapping the 0.266° solar disk across 9 eclipses.
  • Team-Defined Viability Index: 0.35 Sun + 0.25 DTE + 0.15 Joint + 0.15 Dark + 0.10 Slope≤5°.

3 · Cosmic Workspace, 3D Rover Simulator & Candidate Finder

NASA Eyes-Inspired 3D Rover Simulator, Command Split & Two-Mode Discovery

3D Rover · Command Split · 360° Sky · Finder

1. GPU Three.js 3D Terrain & 6-Wheel Rover Simulator

  • WebGL South Pole Mesh (Lunar3DViewport.tsx): 250×250 LOLA surface (±150 km) with 5 overlays (Regolith, Sun, Earth, Slope, Composite), JPL solar shadows, 4,000 stars, 3D Sun corona, Earth DTE beam & 9 A3 polygons.
  • Interactive 6-Wheel Rover (roverPhysics.ts): Drive via WASD / Arrows / HUD pad / click-to-waypoint autopilot over 240 m dem_fine_240m.i16 with live pitch/roll alignment & 1×/10×/50× gears.
  • 4 Camera Modes: Rover Chase, 1st-Person Mast POV reticle, Orbit, & Free Cam / Tactical, plus PSR LED headlights & 160-pt trail.

2. Cosmic UI, Command Split, Sky & 4.1-Year Timeline

  • 5 Workspace Views & Ring Gauges (App.tsx): 3D Rover & Surface hero, synchronized Command Split (3D + 2D Map + 360° Sky), 2D Map & Sky, Compare A vs B, and Candidate Directory.
  • 3-Tab Mission Dock: 14-Day Timeline & Sensitivity (36,216 h, 9 eclipses), 360° Horizon Sky (0.5–50 m ghost mast), & Rover Traverse Profile.
  • 31-Site Compare & Custom Probe: Drop browser ray-marched pins, expand Science & Methodology Notes, & export JSON/CSV briefs.

3. Computed Candidate Finder (site_candidates.json Mode A + Web Worker Mode B)

Mode A (Instant Grid Search): Filters & ranks the precomputed 250×250 (1.2 km) polar grid + 140 refined 5×5 (240 m) summit blocks by Composite Score, Sun %, Earth DTE %, Joint %, or Shortest Night within the full map, Artemis III regions, or radius—enforcing slope & separation rules. Mode B (Live Worker): Scans custom neighborhoods on-demand in scanAroundWorker.ts.

5 · Mission Impact, Scientific Honesty & Roadmap

Practical Value Today & High-Resolution Extensions for Artemis Missions

Honest Engineering Boundaries

Immediate Mission & Educational Impact

  • Zero-Barrier 3D & Data Access: Static browser execution lets engineers, students, and planners drive 3D rover traverses and inspect 36,216 h of LOLA + Horizons geometry with zero server setup.
  • Actionable Trade-Offs: Quantifies how mast height (0.5 m → 50 m) and arrival epoch change solar continuity and DTE communication gaps.
  • Reproducible Briefs: Exports structured JSON/CSV comparison briefs with full provenance metadata and URL state sharing.

Explicit Model Limitations

  • 240 m/px Terrain & Rover Baseline: LDEM_75S_240M does not resolve meter-scale boulders, footpad hazards, or regolith terramechanics; scores and rover tilt reflect exploratory decision-support fidelity, not certification.
  • South Pole DEM Domain: Sites north of 75°S report n/a for DEM terrain stats and use analytic spherical dip.
  • Geometric Scope: Relative array power (%) and DTE omit thermal derating, dust, RF link margins, and Earth atmospheric refraction during eclipses.

Next-Stage Technical Roadmap

  • Multi-Scale LOLA 5 m/px & LROC NAC DTMs: Stream NASA GSFC PGDA 5 mpp tiles into the Three.js mesh for meter-scale local hazard and rover refinement around top Mode A candidates.
  • Lunar Relay Orbiters: Add Lunar Gateway NRHO and elliptical relay ephemerides for far-side and ridge-masked sites.
  • Battery SoC & Energy-Optimal Routing: Couple rover kinematics and hourly array fractions with battery state-of-charge A* pathfinding between PSRs and sunlit ridges.
